OPEC+ weighs larger output hike for December
OPEC+ delegates say the group is weighing a December output increase of up to 400,000 barrels per day, roughly double the 200,000 bpd most analysts expected. Saudi Arabia is pushing for the larger hike to win back market share lost to U.S. and Brazilian producers after months of voluntary cuts, while Iraq and Kazakhstan are resisting. WTI fell 2.4% to $90.34 within an hour of the reports, and Brent slipped below $99. No final decision has been made yet. Watch Tuesday's online ministerial meeting at 18:00 PKT, where the size of the increase should be confirmed.
Why it matters: more supply than priced in, directly pressuring CL.

Freight costs soar as sanctioned tankers sit idle
Rates for shipping crude out of the Black Sea and Baltic have hit their highest level since 2022, with a Suezmax charter now costing about $95,000 a day, up 40% in two weeks. Roughly 70 sanctioned tankers are sitting idle after new U.S. designations, shrinking the fleet able to carry Russian barrels. Traders have asked Washington to let some ships finish voyages already under way. Higher freight is widening the gap between delivered cargo prices and the benchmarks, and is supporting Brent over WTI. Watch for U.S. Treasury guidance on wind-down licenses later this week.
Why it matters: higher shipping costs and fewer available barrels support prices over the coming weeks.

US rig count falls for third straight week
Baker Hughes counted 4 fewer active U.S. oil rigs this week, taking the total to 412, the third straight weekly decline and the lowest since early 2022. The Permian Basin accounted for three of the four. Producers are keeping budgets flat and returning cash to shareholders rather than drilling more, even with WTI near $90. Fewer rigs today usually means slower output growth six to nine months out. The market barely reacted because the drop was expected. Watch next Friday's count and shale companies' quarterly guidance for any change in drilling plans.
Why it matters: slower drilling trims future US supply growth, a mild support for CL.

EIA trims 2027 U.S. crude output forecast
The U.S. Energy Information Administration lowered its 2027 crude production forecast by 50,000 barrels per day to 13.9 million bpd. Prices did not move on the release, since the cut is small and largely priced in. The next monthly outlook is due on Tuesday at 20:30 PKT and may revise demand as well.

Japan's October crude imports fall 6% from a year ago
Japan imported about 2.3 million barrels per day of crude in October, down 6% from a year earlier, according to preliminary customs data. Refiners cut runs for seasonal maintenance and drew on stored oil instead of buying new cargoes. Imports from the Middle East fell the most, while shipments from the U.S. rose slightly. Analysts expect purchases to recover in December as winter heating demand picks up. Watch China's import data later this week for a clearer read on Asian demand.
Why it matters: weaker Asian buying adds mild downward pressure in the short term.

Johan Sverdrup back at full output after power outage
Equinor said Norway's Johan Sverdrup field, which pumps about 755,000 barrels per day, returned to full production after a two-day power outage. The return of these barrels removes a short-term supply worry for European refiners. Watch North Sea loading schedules for December to confirm exports are back to normal.
